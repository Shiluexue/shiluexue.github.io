Design a delightful end-to-end instant messaging experience — 0 to 1.

Better serve recruiters and job seekers in the LinkedIn InCareer app.

“Behind every opportunity is a private conversation.”

As a business that undertakes communication needs across recruiting, job hunting, network building, information sharing, and consulting services, we need a stable, timely, convenient, and friendly functional experience — so we can meet basic communication needs and improve communication efficiency.

Background

A new design adapting to changes

After the sunset of the localized version of LinkedIn, we launched InCareer, a new, standalone jobs application for China.

Messaging aims at connecting professionals with opportunities through trusted and productive private conversations. So the new design of InCareer Messenger was a remarkable opportunity to refine product backlogs and reevaluate the messaging experience based on research.

Leveraging what we have learned

Messenger had gone through usability studies and received suggestions for improvements. I archived product backlogs for future management. Throughout this time, I also conducted a round of messenger audits of the LinkedIn app and set up recurring time with the entire product team to bring forward any UX- or tech-debt items that may negatively impact the user experience.

Messenger audit — 13 findings

Aligning on the goals

The unmet user needs and usability issues were all set. Before beginning any design work, I brought PM, research, and engineering leaders together as part of the messenger audit review — with the goal of agreeing on a unified vision.

Who are we designing for?

LinkedIn Talent Solution Customer

LTS customers can connect with candidates directly via LinkedIn InMail. Need to search, connect, and hire smarter.

Non-LTS Recruiter

Needs to post jobs and connect with candidates through communication.

Job Seeker

Needs to connect and communicate with recruiters efficiently.

Product Goals

  • Meet the interactive scene of job search and recruitment
  • Create communication possibilities such as Job, Dixit Message, and InMail
  • Optimize the underlying interaction experience
  • Respect Chinese user habits and localize

Taking a principled approach

As I began this process, I defined a set of principles to guide us. These have been iterated on as we've learned along the way, but they still serve as our north star.

Entities are not to be multiplied beyond necessity

Our value should always be faster and more lightweight than the alternative. Avoid the pitfall of over-optimizing for customers.

Tailor to context

Understand where people are, what they're doing, and their unmet needs in different contexts.

Meet members where they are

Messenger should be like water — whenever and wherever members need it, it's there. Enable a consistent and seamless compose experience across all apps.

How I got there

First and foremost, I prioritized the UX debt according to the following factors after aligning with PMs and Engineers.

  • Description of the issue from users' standpoint (how is it affecting them?)
  • Where in the experience it occurs (awareness, consideration, conversion)
  • Frequency of occurrence (how often does it happen?)
  • Level of UX and development effort needed to fix the issue (low, medium, or high)

Tracking and prioritizing issues

Theme II · Message Everywhere

Connect more job opportunities through conversation.

To adapt to the new strategy of helping China-based professionals find jobs in China and Chinese companies find quality candidates, we need to build a bridge of connection. Behind every opportunity is a private conversation — Messenger serves as a crucial bridge.

From our former UX research findings and data insights, I found opportunities to help satisfy unmet needs and create more connecting opportunities.

Data learning · Messaging drives retention

  • Messaging job posters of applications drove +9pp week-2 retention of job applicants — 91% of applicants come back the following week.
  • 96% came back the following week among those who sent a chat message to job poster and received a reply within 7 days.

How might we connect opportunities through conversation?

  • Drive messaging from Job and Discover

Theme III · Intuitive and efficient chat experience

Sort out the mess before designing the details.

Messenger is a gathering place for various businesses and contains all kinds of functions. With fragmentary usability issues, UI audits, and a new messenger goal in mind, I needed an organizational structure to sort out — in case of missing functions in this new design.

So before heading into the design, I listed the user behaviors and message types to help me get through the mess.

I leveraged what we learned — the usability issues and consistency issues of the messenger of LinkedIn China.

Chat window design

Members come to LinkedIn for many reasons, leading to opportunities to have conversations. To make it a productive and trusted messaging tool, I nailed the basics and improved the chat experience. The chat bubble changed to light blue to accommodate the highlighted link and echo the new design system.

Auto-converting a link to a card is not always helpful and can't accommodate all scenarios. Sometimes users just want to send a link, but we convert it to a card. So do less when user behavior can not be predicted.

I sorted out the most frequently used scenarios in our app — sharing content, sharing contacts, and job cards. These internal links are automatically converted to cards to better convey information, while external links do not.

Handing off UI spec to developers

To ensure the quality and reducibility of the deliverables, I created a specific UI SPEC and conducted many rounds of UX/UI QA.

After settling the design details, I walked through the checklist and found the whole InCareer was lacking of loading / load-more states. To make the experience smoother, I led the design and implementation of loading across the whole app. I analyzed different scenarios and tailored solutions based on user motivation.

Challenging our assumptions

We successfully shipped InCareer. In the following usability testing, I discussed with UER the 3 key points we wanted to follow up on:

  • Is message design intuitive and trustworthy?
  • Is the DIXIT Job card design unobtrusive, yet noticeable?
  • Do users clearly understand the sending status of the DIXIT job card?

Results

  • From the SEQ questionnaire, the message model scored 6.64 / 7.
  • All 3 assumptions were verified and received positive feedback.
